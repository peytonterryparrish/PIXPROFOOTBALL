## 2. The between-play flow (what is new, in play order)
1. **Whistle**: a player called off the play now decelerates from his running speed (`WalkTo` starts at the sim speed, before it started at 0 and skidded); the tackle aftermath (get-ups, pile) is the CHOREO one; `ServiceAftermath` then walks everybody toward the guessed next huddle spot (`HuddleGuess`: the offense 8.2 yd behind the dead-ball spot, the defense 8.8 yd in front of it; a turnover swaps) after 1.4 s, the men standing at the pile after 2.2 s.
2. **Situational reactions** (`PlayStage.Scenes.cs`, deterministic from the play number): the first-down point (the referee's arm-forward clip on the upper body, 55 % of first downs), the receiver's frustration on an incompletion (36 %), the passer's after a sack (50 %), a cheer from the tackler and his nearest teammate on a fourth-down stop or turnover on downs (70 %). Per game (3-minute quarters): 7 points, 8 frustrations, 8 cheers.
3. **Spot and measurement**: the referee's crew already spotted the ball; officials no longer stand inside players (`SeparateFromPlayers`, B-029); a play that ends within 0.42 yd of the line to gain is measured (banner and 2.6 s more, 2 a game here, NFL 2 to 3).
4. **Next play built with the same bodies** (`PlayStage.PrepareCarry` / `Begin`): no cut to black. A returning player starts where he stands (matched by player id), a substitute starts at his team's bench and jogs on, a player who is not in the next lineup walks to the bench and stands on the sideline (up to six, `SidelinePlayers`) and jogs on again when his number is called. Personnel changes (11 / 12 / 21 groups, nickel, special-teams units, turnovers) show as substitutions for free: 857 jog-ons a game.
5. **Huddle**: the offense (quarterback leading, `huddle_leader`) 8 yd behind the ball, the defense a short one around the middle linebacker, both break together after 2.4 s / 1.5 s and walk to the line through the pre-snap glide; a substitute still on his way does not hold the huddle; the snap waits for the break (`BreakInProgress`). Hurry-up (CPU, `PlayCaller.IsHurryUp`) and special teams skip the huddle.
6. **Timeouts** (CPU, both teams, watch mode): both teams jog to their sideline and gather round the coaches (huddle idle), 3 s after the last man arrives, then walk back; 6 a game. **Quarter and half breaks**: the bodies stay for the banner; at halftime all 22 leave for the benches and the second half comes on from them (B-026: the break used to show an empty field).
7. **Clock**: in a simulated game with a short quarter the clock is compressed instead of the plays cut (`GameState.ClockScale = quarter minutes / 15`, `NormClock` for every late-game rule), a hurry-up burns 9 s between snaps (B-032 / B-035), the two-minute warning stops the clock at 2:00, a kickoff costs no clock until the receiving team has the ball (B-072).

## 4. Game quality (B-007, the CPU offense that cannot score)
Findings, in order of size:
1. **Short quarters cut the snaps, not the clock.** 33 plays in a 3-minute-quarter game is 16 per team (an NFL team has 63): 0-7 is a natural score. `ClockScale` gives a 3-minute-quarter game the same number of snaps as a 15-minute one (about 125 scrimmage plays, 180 with kicks); scores follow (headless: 21 to 23 points per team at 61 snaps).
2. **Star effect.** The league run in the lab (32 teams x 5 games): one elite back made Miami 11.7 yards a carry, New Orleans 10.4, Houston 8.1; league range 3.2 to 11.7, points per team sd 10.5; Buffalo v Miami was 10-46. Cause: the tackle contest used the raw rating gap (tackler minus carrier, 20 to 30 points for a star), so the star broke every tackle. `SimTuning.TackleGapScale = 0.55` (the career abilities keep full strength): league 20.5 points, 4.5 yards a carry, range 2.9 to 6.4, sd 7.3 (NFL about 6). Buffalo now scores (17 points in game 1, 25 in the earlier run of game 2); Miami with its 86 / 91 / 87 stars is still the best team by a lot.
3. **Pass pro always shed** (B-030 / B-036): `SimTuning.PassBias` 0 -> 0.06 (an even pair no longer sheds after the 0.9 s commit every time): sheds per pass play 1.79 -> 1.56, sacks 2.9 -> 2.5 a team (NFL 2.3).
4. Headless calibration (40 games, `FortyCpuGames`): points 21.9 to 23.0 per team, 61 plays, rush 123 to 127, pass 196 to 204 net, completion 62 to 66 %, sacks 1.9 to 2.1, int 0.65, fumbles lost 0.65, penalties 5.3, punt average 45.7 to 46.0.
