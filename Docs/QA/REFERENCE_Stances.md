## QB mechanics (images 68–82) — the rules the QB animation must follow
**Under center (69, 80 coaching card)**
- Feet shoulder-width apart, weight on the balls of the feet, slightly pigeon-toed. If the feet are staggered, the toes still point in.
- Back straight, with roughly 45° angles between back, thighs and knees. Head up and on a swivel. The cadence is loud and commanding.
- Hands under the center, thumbs together (right thumb in the left thumb pocket for a right-hander). The top hand presses up into the center and the bottom hand catches the ball. Hips drop about 6 in on "Go" so the hands stay under the center.
- The ball arrives in the middle of the hands; the QB secures it into the belly and opens to the drop.

**Shotgun (77)**
- Center in a two-hand snap stance. QB 5–7 yd back, knees flexed, hands ready at the chest, eyes on the defense.

**Pocket carriage (68, 75, 78)**
- Base just wider than the shoulders (B), knees bent and balanced, slight forward lean (L).
- Ball held high at the chest or armpit, in both hands, with the point up (U). Elbows in. The feet keep a light, active bounce while the eyes scan.

**Throwing motion (72, 74, 79, 81, 82)**
- Front shoulder closed toward the target, front arm extended and pointing at it.
- The ball comes up by the ear with the elbow at or above shoulder height.
- The front foot steps toward the target. The hips rotate before the shoulders. The release is high, with the follow-through across the body and the throwing hand finishing near the opposite hip.
- At release the spine is near vertical (about 90°) and the drive leg is angled about 54–57° (82): a long stride and a back leg that drives the throw.

**Front knee (73)**
- The front knee must stay stable over the foot. It must never collapse inward. The planted foot points at the target.

**Moving and on the run (70, 76)**
- Throws on the move keep the ball high. Shoulders turn to the target even while running. Off-platform throws (76) are a separate animation set from set-feet throws.

## WR & CB stances (images 83–95)
**Wide receiver, two-point split stance (83–87, 90)**
- Inside foot forward, outside foot back. The stagger is about one foot-length; the front foot is on the line, toes straight ahead.
- Weight about 70% on the front foot, on the ball of the foot. The back heel is off the ground and the back leg is loaded.
- Knees bent and hips low. The back is flat or slightly forward; the chest is over the front knee.
- Hands relaxed at chest or thigh height: one hand ready near the front knee, the other near the hip.
- Head turned inside to watch the ball (the snap), never looking at the defender.
- Still before the snap: no swaying. The first step on the snap comes from the back-foot push, with no false step.

**Cornerback, off or zone (91–95)**
- Square or slightly turned to the receiver, at 5–8 yd depth. Feet about shoulder-width, often slightly staggered (the outside foot back for an outside-leverage technique).
- Knees bent, hips low ("in the chair"). Weight on the balls of the feet, ready to backpedal. Back flat and leaning forward.
- Hands relaxed in front of the thighs or knees. Eyes on the receiver's hips or the QB, depending on the coverage.

**Cornerback, press-man (88, 89)**
- Head and eyes up, with a naturally flat back.
- Feet armpit-width apart, squared up, toes straight. Knees squeezed slightly in, bent so the weight is on the balls of the feet.
- Hands up and ready in front of the chest to jam ("relaxed chest, bent at the waist"). Directly across from the receiver, 0–1 yd off.

**Common**
- Neither WRs nor CBs ever stand straight-legged before the snap.
- Small idle motion is allowed: breathing, a slight weight shift, a hand-fidget. Large swaying is not.

## Line stances (images 96–101)
**OL 3-point (98, and 56/65 earlier)**
- Feet just wider than shoulder-width with a slight stagger: the inside foot up, the outside foot back by about toe-to-instep. Weight on the balls of the feet.
- One hand down, on the same side as the back foot, slightly inside the back foot. Light pressure for a pass set, more for a run block. The other forearm rests on the thigh, or the hand hangs ready.
- Back flat, parallel to the ground. Hips level with or slightly above the shoulders. Head up, eyes through the defender.
- **Stance tells (98):** weight forward with the down hand pressing the ground (run). Sitting back with a light hand (pass, screen or draw). A slight lean in the pull direction (pulling). Keep these subtle; they're a realism detail, not a giveaway.

**OL 2-point (97, 101)**, used in shotgun or pass-heavy sets
- Feet about shoulder-width, slight stagger, knees bent, hips low ("sitting in a chair").
- Back flat or slightly upright. Hands ready at the knees or thighs, or up and inside. Head up.
- Balanced so the lineman can kick-slide or drive-block.

**DL 3- or 4-point (96, 99, 100)**
- Hand down in front of the shoulder with fingertips loaded; the 4-point stance has both hands down. Feet staggered, the back foot on the down-hand side.
- Hips higher than the shoulders (weight forward for get-off). Back flat and head up, eyes on the ball or the blocker's hands.
- Coiled for first-step explosion. The free hand rests by the knee and thigh, ready to strike.
- Edge rushers may stand in a 2-point or wide-9 stance at an angle to the tackle.

**Common**
- Linemen sit still from set to snap; only the stance tell and breathing show.
- After the snap the lineman explodes out of the stance in one fluid first step. There's no pop from the stance pose into the run cycle.

## DL stances & alignment techniques (images 102–108)
**4-point stance (102, 106, 108)**: goal-line, short yardage and interior penetrators
- Both hands down, roughly shoulder-width and slightly ahead of the shoulders, fingertips loaded. Feet staggered or square.
- Hips high, above the shoulders. Back flat, head up. All the weight is forward, built for low and explosive get-off.

**3-point stance (103, 104)**: base DT and DE
- One hand down. The back foot is on the down-hand side, with a heel-to-toe stagger.
- Hips slightly higher than the shoulders. The free hand is loaded by the thigh, ready to strike. The eyes are on the ball or the blocker's hands.
- Edge players angle their body slightly toward the QB (a "tilted" or wide-9 alignment).

**Alignment techniques (105)**, which the DL position must respect relative to the OL
- **Even numbers are head-up:** 0 = center, 2 = guard, 4 = tackle, 6 = tight end, 8 = wing.
- **Odd numbers are outside shade:** 1 = center/A gap, 3 = guard, 5 = tackle, 7 = tight end, 9 = wide.
- **"i" is inside shade:** 2i = guard, 4i = tackle, 6i = tight end.
- A 4-3 under front is 1-tech NT, 3-tech DT, 5-tech and 7/9 DEs. A 3-4 is 0-tech NT with 4/5-tech DEs.

**Gap control (107)**: DL assignments map to gaps (A/B/C/D), and slants or stunts move from the alignment technique into an assigned gap.

## RB stance & ball-carrier movement (images 109–118)
**RB stances (109, 110)**
- **2-point (I-form tailback, pistol, shotgun offset):** feet shoulder-width, square or slightly staggered. Hands resting on the thighs just above the knees, back flat or slightly forward, head up, eyes on the defense. Weight balanced on the balls of the feet so he can go either way.
- **3-point (fullback, goal line):** balanced, feet shoulder-width, back flat, head up, one hand down lightly.
- **Shotgun RB beside the QB:** upright-ish 2-point, hands on the knees, ready for the mesh or blitz pickup.

**Carrying the ball (112, 113, 116, 118)**
- Ball high and tight with the "five points of pressure": fingertips over the point, forearm, bicep, ribs and chest. Carried in the arm AWAY from the nearest defender (switch hands when cutting across the field).
- Free arm pumps naturally or is out for balance or a stiff-arm. Nose over the toes and a forward body lean when accelerating. Hips low through contact and cuts.

**Cuts & moves (114, 117)**
- Plant on the outside foot, sink the hips, and drive off the plant foot. The ball switches to the outside arm.
- Shoulders stay square-ish; the head and eyes lead the cut. No speed-preserving pivot: some speed is lost in the plant, then regained.

**Hurdle (115)**: lead leg bent high, trail leg tucked, ball tucked tight. Only over a low, diving defender.

**Open-field running (111, 117, 118)**: high knee drive, full arm swing on the free arm. Feet land under the hips (no overstriding), with visible weight transfer and ground contact every stride. This is the cure for "floating/gliding" (playtest note 26).
